01 — The problem

The hardest part isn't building anymore. It's getting the right people to care.

AI, no-code and increasingly accessible development have made software dramatically easier to build. The cost of building collapsed. The cost of being noticed did not.

None of that solved the questions that actually decide whether anyone buys. Those questions are still yours, and they still have to be answered in a specific order.

This book was built to answer those questions — in the right order.

02 — The diagnosis

No leads
→ Run ads.
No replies
→ Try outbound.
Low conversion
→ Change the website.
Deals stall
→ Hire sales.

But the problem
is almost always
upstream.

If the upstream pieces are wrong, more activity doesn't fix the system — it just makes the wrong system more expensive. Positioning feeds the ICP, the ICP feeds the message, the message feeds every channel, and the channels feed the numbers.

So the first question isn't which tactic. It's which part of the engine is actually broken — which is exactly where the book starts, with Worksheet 1: The Honest Diagnostic.

11 — Questions

Before you buy.

Who is this book for?
Early-stage B2B SaaS founders, from zero to roughly $1–2M ARR, with a small team, no marketing or sales hire, and no repeatable way to create pipeline. It assumes you can build — and that you have never done go-to-market before.
Is this only for B2B SaaS?
It is written specifically for B2B software. The book is explicit that if you sell to consumers, most of the mechanics here don't transfer.
What if I don't have customers yet?
That's a supported starting point. The book assumes you may be at zero, and the first 90-day block is built around finding out what is true: conversations, the positioning interview script, and an honest look at whoever has engaged with you so far.
What if I already have some traction?
It still applies up to roughly $1–2M ARR — you'll use it to make the position, ICP, message and pipeline model explicit rather than implicit. If you are at $10M ARR with a VP of Sales, the book says plainly: read the benchmark appendix and skip the rest.
Is this a marketing book or a sales book?
Both, deliberately. It covers positioning, ICP, message and channels alongside founder-led selling — discovery, demos, qualification, mutual action plans and closing — because at this stage they are one job.
How practical is it?
Every chapter ends with something you do. There are 14 worksheets, and Chapter 12 sequences the whole book into a week-by-week 90-day plan. Fill them in and you finish with a written GTM plan rather than a feeling of inspiration.
What are the 14 worksheets?
The Honest Diagnostic · Positioning Canvas · ICP Definition & Account List · Messaging Map · PMF Scorecard · Mutual Action Plan · Pricing Decisions · Motion Selection · Channel Test Card · GTM Model, One Page · Automation Audit · Sales Playbook Contents · 90-Day Tracker · Quarterly GTM Review.
What benchmark data is included?
Appendix B carries 2026 benchmark tables: funnel conversion rates, win rate by opportunity source, sales cycle and recommended pipeline coverage by ACV, cost per lead and per opportunity, net dollar retention, contract length, quota attainment, and the pricing and motion mix. The principal source is a January 2026 survey of GTM executives at 150+ B2B software companies, plus published research from Gartner and the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute. Every figure names its source and sample — they are directional context, not targets.
How long is the book?
99 pages, 13 chapters, four parts and four appendices, including the worksheets, the benchmark tables, a glossary and a sources list.
What format is it?
PDF — readable on desktop, tablet or phone, and the worksheets can be printed or rebuilt in whatever tool you actually use.
